We study unbalanced unit-circle incidences through the top-degree mass Lambda_k(n), the largest possible sum of the k largest degrees in a unit-distance graph of at most n planar points. For k <= n/2, this quantity agrees within a factor of 2 with the maximum number of incidences between n points and k unit circles with arbitrary centres. We compare its growth across norms for 1 <= k <= n. It is Theta(kn) when the unit circle contains a segment and O(n^(2/3) k^(2/3) + n) for strictly convex norms. An aspect-ratio-dependent grid construction in Valtr's norm, together with the linear lower bound, matches the latter bound for every k. Thus strict convexity alone cannot yield an improvement at any scale. For norms outside a meagre set, a theorem of Alon, Bucic and Sauermann instead bounds the top-degree mass between linear and O(n log n).

As an application, we characterize the minimax expected error of unit-distance counting under epsilon-node differential privacy as Theta(Lambda_ceil(1/epsilon)(n)) for every epsilon in (0,1]. In the Euclidean plane this gives error Theta(n) for epsilon >= n^(-1/2), a factor 1/epsilon below the standard Laplace mechanism. Polynomially superlinear error at some privacy levels is equivalent to a polynomially superlinear unit-distance extremal function, as established in 2026. The remaining Euclidean range leads to an open problem on unbalanced incidences.
